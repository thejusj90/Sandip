WELLBEING & CULTURE

Breathing for Better Teams and Outcomes

Better teamwork is not only about processes and tools. Explore how brief breathing and awareness practices may help people regulate stress and respond more deliberately at work.

Most organisations try to improve teamwork by changing what people do.

New processes.

New collaboration tools.

New meeting structures.

New KPIs.

New leadership frameworks.

Those things matter.

But there is another variable inside every meeting that receives far less attention:

the state of the people in the room.

The short answer

Breathing exercises will not magically create a high-performing team. However, research suggests that breath-based and relaxation practices may help reduce perceived stress for some employees. Creating brief moments for individual regulation before demanding interactions may therefore support better conditions for listening, communication and deliberate decision-making.

The distinction is important.

Breathing does not replace good leadership.

It can help people arrive differently to the conversation.

Teams do not leave their nervous systems at the door

Consider two versions of the same Monday meeting.

In the first:

someone arrives from another difficult meeting.

Another person is answering Slack messages.

The team leader is thinking about a deadline.

Someone feels defensive about last week's numbers.

The meeting begins immediately.

In the second:

everyone takes perhaps 30 seconds to arrive.

Screens stop moving.

People notice their bodies.

One or two comfortable breaths.

Then the conversation starts.

The agenda is identical.

The people are identical.

But the starting state is different.

That difference can influence how people experience what comes next.

Stress changes how we communicate

Under pressure, our attention can narrow.

We may become more impatient.

Listen less carefully.

Interpret ambiguous comments negatively.

Rush towards decisions.

None of this necessarily means somebody is a bad colleague.

They may simply be operating under considerable physiological and cognitive load.

Workplace research provides evidence that stress-management interventions incorporating relaxation and breathing can reduce measures of perceived stress in some settings. One randomised workplace trial involving 152 employees used relaxation breathing and progressive muscle relaxation over eight weeks and reported improvements in perceived stress.

That does not prove that breathing creates better business outcomes directly.

But it gives organisations a useful question:

What happens when people learn to regulate themselves before trying to regulate everyone else?

The 60-second meeting reset

This does not require turning the boardroom into a wellness retreat.

At the beginning of an important meeting:

Put devices down.

Sit comfortably.

Allow the shoulders to relax.

Notice one natural inhalation.

Notice the exhalation.

Take another comfortable breath.

Then begin.

No performance.

No spiritual language required.

No demand that everybody feel calm.

The purpose is simply to mark the transition from whatever happened before the meeting to what is happening now.

Use breathing before moments that matter

Breathing practices may be especially useful around transitions.

Before:

  • difficult performance conversations;
  • negotiations;
  • leadership meetings;
  • client presentations;
  • conflict-resolution discussions;
  • high-stakes decisions;
  • public speaking;
  • brainstorming sessions.

The objective is not to remove pressure.

Pressure is often unavoidable.

The objective is to notice your state before allowing that state to determine your behaviour.

Regulation is not the same as relaxation

This may be the most important distinction.

Workplaces do not always need everybody to be relaxed.

Sometimes a situation requires intensity.

Speed.

Urgency.

Assertiveness.

Regulation means having greater capacity to recognise your state and respond appropriately.

A leader who is angry may still need to confront a problem.

But there is a difference between:

"I am angry, therefore I will react,"

and

"I notice that I am angry. What does this situation require from me?"

That tiny distinction can shape culture over time.

Better teams begin with better moments

Culture sounds like a large abstract concept.

In reality, culture is expressed through thousands of small interactions.

How someone responds to a mistake.

Whether a colleague feels heard.

What happens when targets are missed.

How disagreement is handled.

Whether leaders create fear or clarity under pressure.

No breathing technique can solve structural problems, poor management or unhealthy working conditions.

Nor should it be used to ask employees to calmly tolerate environments that need fixing.

But alongside good organisational practices, breathing and awareness can offer individuals a simple capability:

notice the reaction before choosing the response.

And when enough people learn to do that consistently, meetings may begin to feel different.

Then teams may begin to work differently too.

Frequently asked questions

Can breathing exercises reduce workplace stress?

Some controlled studies have reported reductions in perceived stress following workplace interventions involving breathing or relaxation practices. The evidence is promising but varies by intervention and population.

How long does a workplace breathing exercise need to be?

Formal research programmes often use longer and repeated practices, so a 30- or 60-second pause should not be presented as equivalent to those interventions. For teams, however, a brief pause can still function as a practical awareness ritual before a meeting.

Can breathing improve team performance?

There is stronger evidence around stress and individual wellbeing than around direct improvements in team performance. It is more accurate to say that regulation practices may create conditions that support more deliberate communication and decision-making rather than claiming they automatically improve performance.

Should companies introduce breathwork programmes?

They can be offered as one optional wellbeing or self-regulation practice, but they should complement, never replace, good workload design, psychological safety, effective leadership and healthy organisational systems.
