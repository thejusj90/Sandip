## Two ways to start {#services}

### Community Impact Strategy

Our flagship engagement starts with one organisation, one defined community and one outcome that matters to both. We listen to affected groups, examine what the organisation can influence and compare practical routes to change. Together we outline a theory of change: what the project will do, how that could lead to the intended outcome and which assumptions need testing.

**What you receive:** an agreed outcome statement, stakeholder priorities and differences, options appraisal, costed pilot brief, ownership plan and baseline and measurement plan.

### Decision Impact Review

Use this before committing to a product feature, development, workplace policy, visitor experience or public programme. We compare options, including keeping things as they are where relevant. We map who may benefit, who could bear costs, the risks and opportunities that deserve attention, and the strength of the available evidence. We can flag potential environmental effects for further investigation, such as resource use, waste or pressure on a site.

**What you receive:** a decision brief, options comparison, stakeholder findings, impact and uncertainty register, and recommended actions with owners.

## From strategy to delivery

### Impact Readiness Scan

A focused first engagement for teams with a broad idea or an unclear brief. Identify who needs to be heard, what evidence is missing and which next step would be useful.

**What you receive:** a readiness note, information requests and a recommended scope.

### Pilot Design and Implementation Partnership

Translate the strategy into a workable pilot with a defined audience, activities, partners, budget and review dates. Delivery support and monitoring can be commissioned in stages. The scope can include stakeholder facilitation and training for the people who will own the work locally.

**What you receive:** a pilot plan, roles and budget, baseline tools and a review schedule.

### Baseline, Pilot Evaluation and Monitoring

Establish what is happening before a pilot, track delivery and review early outcomes, including differences between groups and unexpected effects. We distinguish what was delivered from what changed and what the evidence can credibly attribute to the pilot.

**What you receive:** an indicator plan, monitoring summary and a recommendation to continue, adapt, stop or scale. If Dīpātma designed or delivered the work, we disclose that role and recommend independent evaluation when the commissioner or decision warrants it.

## Apply the work to a real decision

### Hospitality and destinations

A resort wants a visitor experience that creates paid opportunities for nearby residents. Test which roles residents want, how payment and scheduling would work, who might be excluded and how the operator would know whether the arrangement is worth continuing. Explore [hospitality wellness consulting](/solutions/hospitality-and-retreats/).

### Developers and facility operators

A developer wants a shared amenity that more residents can use. Compare layout, hours, access costs and management options with users and non-users before the design is locked in. Explore [wellness facility consulting](/solutions/wellness-spaces-and-facilities/).

### Businesses and institutions

A business plans to launch a feature or workplace policy. Review where benefits and burdens may fall, which alternatives reduce foreseeable problems and what early signals should trigger a redesign. Explore [corporate and SME solutions](/solutions/corporates-and-smes/).

### NGOs, foundations and public programme teams

A team wants to improve access rather than report attendance alone. Define the barrier with the people experiencing it, test a targeted change and measure repeat use and accessibility alongside participation. Explore [NGO and community well-being programmes](/solutions/ngos-and-social-impact/).

These examples illustrate the kind of brief we can scope. They are not commissioned projects or claims of achieved outcomes.

## How the Dīpātma Method applies

1. **Insight:** Agree on the decision or outcome and listen to affected groups, including people who may be overlooked.
2. **Architecture:** Compare options, make the theory of change explicit and design a costed pilot.
3. **Activation:** Establish the baseline, test the design and record how the pilot is delivered.
4. **Continuity:** Assign ownership, prepare partners and plan what support will remain when the pilot ends.
5. **Evidence:** Review outcomes and unintended effects, explain uncertainty and agree whether to continue, adapt, stop or scale.

Learn more about [the Dīpātma Method](/approach/) or read [how to design a community impact pilot](/insights/designing-a-community-impact-pilot/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How is this different from Dīpātma’s NGO and Social Impact portfolio?

The NGO portfolio focuses on delivering and sustaining community well-being programmes and developing local capability. Impact Advisory can be commissioned by any sector when a decision or desired outcome needs investigation, design or evaluation. A review can end with a recommendation even if Dīpātma will not deliver the project.

### Is this a statutory environmental or social impact assessment?

No. Dīpātma offers non-statutory advisory and can flag issues that need further assessment. We do not issue environmental clearances, statutory impact assessments, ESG assurance, certifications or legal approvals. Specialist assessment can be scoped separately with appropriately qualified providers.

### Can you work with a CSR sponsor or a public programme team?

We can discuss programme design, stakeholder engagement or pilot learning in an advisory capacity. This does not establish CSR eligibility, fulfil a mandated assessment or replace the commissioning organisation’s procurement and approval requirements.

### How do engagements work commercially?

Start with a fixed-scope scan, a Decision Impact Review or the flagship Community Impact Strategy. Pilot delivery, community participation costs, specialist work and ongoing monitoring are scoped separately. An initial discussion does not imply an outcome or funding commitment.
